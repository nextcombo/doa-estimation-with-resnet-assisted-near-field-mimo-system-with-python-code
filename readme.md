# DOA Estimation with ResNet Assisted Near-Field MIMO System [with Python Code]

Abstract: The near-field effects of the near-to-far multiple input multiple output (MIMO) systems pose numerous challenges to direction of arrival (DoA) estimation. Traditional estimation methods typically assume a far-field wavefront as a plane wave front. This paper investigates the DoA estimation problem in short-range MIMO communication, where the near-field spherical wave impact is significant and cannot be ignored. It transforms this into a regression task and proposes a new framework for near-field DoA estimation in near-to-far MIMO communication systems based on complex value deep learning (CVDL). Under the assumption of a spherical wave model, the array steering vector is determined by both distance and direction. However, solving this regression task with a large number of variables is challenging due to the need to capture a vast array of complex feature representations in the dataset. To address this, a virtual covariance matrix (VCM) is constructed based on received signal data, allowing these features extracted from VCM to handle the intricate coupling between direction and distance. Despite the advancements driven by future wireless big data under communication networks, learning algorithms for complex valued signals are still being developed. Given the inherent one-dimensional structure of signal subspace vectors, this paper introduces a one-dimensional residual network that can directly process complex valued features. Additionally, an antenna matching strategy based on the cropped vector space model is proposed, which can adapt to different antenna sizes and fully leverage complex valued information. Finally, through simulation experiments, the CVDL method is validated to outperform baseline methods in terms of DoA estimation accuracy.
Introduction With the advancement of key enabling technologies in fifth-generation (5G) mobile communication, such as millimeter wave (mmWave), small cells, and massive multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO), direction of arrival (DoA) estimation has attracted extensive research in recent years [1]. Over the past decades, numerous DoA estimation methods have been proposed, ranging from subspace-based techniques [3]-[5], maximum likelihood estimators [6], compression-sensing approaches [7] to hybrid methods [8]-[10]. Effective and accurate DoA estimation algorithms are crucial for large-scale MIMO systems. Specifically, accurate DoA information is vital for beamforming design in large-scale MIMO systems [14].
Most of the aforementioned DoA estimation methods assume far-field plane waves. However, this assumption may be ineffective for future mobile systems. Millimeter wave massive MIMO technology is expected to become an enabler technology for 5G and higher mobile networks, supporting high data rate communication. Nevertheless, the characteristics of short wavelengths and large aperture antennas pose challenges to modeling the propagation directional pattern. When considering Rayleigh distance, the last order scatterer falls within the near field region of the receiving antenna, where spherical wave propagation is applicable [15]. On the other hand, near-field DoA estimation seems to be crucial in many other applications, including vehicle-to-everything (V2X) communication [16], [17], virtual reality, smart homes, and autonomous driving. Among them, landmark signal DoA estimation is the core technology for vehicle positioning, and it is an important branch of the Internet of Things (IoT of Vehicles, or IoTV) [18].
In near-field DOA estimation, the joint estimation of distance and angle is inevitable, which expands the search space and increases the search overhead. To overcome this issue, a subspace method without multidimensional search was proposed in [19]. These subspace methods require multiple snapshots and high signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) to improve spatial resolution. Additionally, an iterative estimation method based on maximum likelihood was proposed for cases with few snapshots and low SNR [20], but as the dimensionality of parameters increases, the iterative method inevitably faces the challenge of slow processing speed.
Meanwhile, wireless networks are generating a large amount of data, and we can use state-of-the-art machine learning (ML) techniques to extract useful information from it [21]-[24]. There have been some efforts to explore the application of ML in DOA estimation. In [25], near-field DOA estimation is modeled as a classification problem based on Support Vector Machines. The authors in [26] extended the application of deep neural networks (DNN) to general far-field acoustic DOA estimation problems, which are modeled as classification problems and not suitable for near-field estimation. Although these ML-based classification methods are interesting, their accuracy depends on the quantization resolution of angles. In [27], an SVR method for estimating near-field sources' DOA was proposed. Popular deep learning (DL) methods are also used for far-field DOA estimation, such as in the field of acoustic source direction finding [28]. However, this acoustic DOA estimation model cannot be directly applied to signal processing in wireless systems. Moreover, in [29], a real-valued DNN (RVNN) was designed for estimating DOA in mixed large-scale MIMO channels. Traditionally, complex signals are divided into true and imaginary parts, which are fed into the network as true values [26], [29]-[31]. However, this processing may not capture the correlation between the true and imaginary parts, leading to phase information loss.
The majority of existing DL methods focus on real-world domains. However, many unresolved wireless communication problems are in complex domains. Combining the concept of CVNN with wireless signal processing can provide further momentum for achieving intelligent radios. CVDL methods refer to deep neural networks (DNN) that can perform complex operations, where the weights of CVNN are complex valued [32], [33]. The authors in [34]-[36] explore the application of CVDL in image and speech processing. These network architectures were designed for image classification, music transcription, or spectrogram prediction. In this context, our motivation can be explained from the following aspects:
Due to the traditional near-field methods requiring high signal-to-noise ratio and fast-sampling or online iterative estimation, it is unlikely to achieve real-time DoA estimation. Moreover, there is a very limited research design of DL methods for near-field DoA estimation. Since DL is a powerful tool for solving difficult nonlinear problems, if we train an end-to-end network to solve near-field DoA estimation, we can improve the robustness of performance. R-space spectrum search and ML-based classification methods using coarse grids cannot meet the precision requirements. Additionally, RVNs may disrupt the structure of complex data by splitting real and imaginary parts. Therefore, regression models based on CVNN are expected to solve these issues. When considering near-field signals, due to the coupling between DoA and distance parameters, DoA estimation based on CVDL is not direct. How to design input functions, activation functions, and loss metrics to encourage us to pursue a general CVDL-based DoA estimation framework.
B. Main Contributions
This paper proposes a novel CVDL-assisted DoA estimation method for near-field MIMO systems, treating DoA estimation as an end-to-end regression task rather than a classification problem. Initially, to decouple distance and direction, we design a new feature representation based on the reconstruction of a virtual covariance matrix (VCM). This representation method eliminates the learning barriers in DoA estimation. Subsequently, a complex feature learning is proposed using a ResNet with complex value residuals. Compared to RVNN, this network avoids the trivial splitting and splicing between real and imaginary parts. To our knowledge, this is the first time that a phase mapping activation function has been developed for a CVNN. How to incorporate CVDL into MIMO systems has not been well addressed before.
The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:
From the perspective of maximum likelihood, this study investigates the problem of near-field DoA estimation, treating it as a process of extracting phase features from input data. Without the need for intricate grid-based classification, we have trained an end-to-end deep network for angle estimation. This network can directly handle complex tasks involving complex values. Based on the reconstructed VCM instead of the entire or half-covariance matrix, we design new features as inputs to the CVNN. As a result, the computational complexity of our deep network is significantly reduced. Moreover, we convert the joint estimation of near-field two-dimensional parameters into a one-dimensional DoA estimation problem, designing input features that eliminate the complex coupling between distance and direction. We propose a convolutional ResNet capable of processing complex one-dimensional features. To ensure training stability, we design a residual block structure with bypass connections. To map the complex output from the real value, we design a phase mapping activation function specifically for the DoA estimation task. Additionally, based on the distribution of the dataset, we carefully select different complex value activation functions in corresponding neuron layers. For our specific regression task, we compare the training results using different loss metrics and determine the appropriate loss function through comparison. The results are that prediction accuracy has been improved. To achieve a general CVNN model applicable to different antenna sizes, this paper proposes a scheme based on cropped VCM. This scheme can be applied to any antenna size without the need for redesigning a new network. Furthermore, since the input is cropped to a fixed shape, it reduces computational costs.
The rest of this paper is organized as follows: In Section 2, we establish the near-field signal model. In Section 3, we propose a distance parameter reconstruction method based on VCM, eliminating the pollution of distance parameters on the signal subspace and propose a feature preprocessing scheme based on VCM. In Section 4, we introduce the DoA estimation framework assisted by CVDL and introduce a complex ResNet with specific model settings. Our simulation results are provided in Section 5, indicating that CVNN outperforms baseline methods. Our conclusions are presented in Section 6.
